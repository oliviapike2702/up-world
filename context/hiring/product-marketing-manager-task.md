## The situation

The Marketing Accelerator is our 12-week leadership course for marketing managers going for a head
of role. It runs twice a year, in March and September. Six modules, each with a lecture and a group
coaching session, an IRL kick-off day, and a graduation dinner at the end. It costs £1,490 exc VAT.

We target 25 paid places every cohort. Here is what we have actually sold:

| Cohort | Paid |
|---|---|
| H1 2023 | 18 |
| H2 2023 | 19 |
| H1 2024 | 18 |
| H2 2024 | 16 |
| H1 2025 | 21 |
| March 2026 | 22 |

Six cohorts, and we have never hit the target. We think 40 is possible. You've just joined as
Product Marketing Manager, and the March 2027 cohort is yours. Doors open in November.

## What we do now, and what it produces

**The launch runs over about 12 weeks.** It starts with warm-lead outreach in December, doors open
in January, and it closes with a public deadline in early March followed by a quiet week of
hustling.

**Email.** Around twelve sends per launch: a warm-pool opener, a development plan email, doors open,
talk promotions, a segmented split between "train your team" for seniors and "time to level up" for
mid-levels, a places-remaining email, graduate stories, last week, last day. The development plan
email is our best performer at 70% opens.

**Two live talks from Lottie, our founder.** These are our strongest conversion tool. We target 130
sign-ups each. In 2026, talk one got 108 sign-ups, 45 people live at peak and 30 still there at the
end. Talk two had 77 sign-ups ten days out.

**Paid ads on Meta, about £4,000 a cohort**, out of a total cohort budget of roughly £9,450. Two
campaigns: a quiz for data and retargeting, and direct response. The quiz produced 50 to 99
completions a week at £3 to £4.50 each and almost none of them applied. Direct response cost per
lead came down from £95 to about £31 once it was fixed. A founder-filmed video ad drove five of nine
direct applications in its first week.

**LinkedIn.** Posts from the company page, the team, and Lottie's personal account, plus direct
messages at scale to marketing managers.

**Warm pools we start every launch with:** about 30 hot leads who nearly joined last time, about 570
warm leads who are past applicants or quiz completers, and about 1,100 who have clicked a previous
email.

**How people buy.** They apply through a Typeform, which creates a deal in HubSpot. We qualify on job
title, message them on WhatsApp to book a 10 to 15 minute phone interview, interview them, send an
acceptance and a payment link, then chase.

## The task

Get us to 40 paid places on the March 2027 cohort.

We'd like you to share:

**1. Your read on why we're stuck at around 20.** What do you think is actually holding this back,
and what would you want to know that isn't in the numbers above? It's fine to land on a view and
state what you'd need to confirm it.

**2. A prioritised plan from now to launch.** Bullets are fine. What you'd do first, what you'd stop
doing, and what you'd protect. We're more interested in the order and the reasoning than the detail.

**3. One piece of the actual work.** Either the first email of the launch, or the concept for the
ad. Your choice. We want to see you make something, not just plan it.

**4. The magic wand version.** No budget, no constraints, nobody telling you no. Tell us the
completely ridiculous idea. This is our favourite part, so please don't skip it.

**5. A Loom, 5 to 8 minutes,** walking us through it. Imagine you're presenting to the Up World team.

## Notes on how this was built (Sept 2026)

- **The six-cohort history is the spine of the task.** "We have never hit 25" is a more honest and
  more interesting prompt than "get us to 40", and it's the fact a good candidate will want to
  explain. Anyone who doesn't engage with it has skimmed.
- **Deliberately left out:** early bird pricing, because it's being phased out and adds noise, and
  cohort feedback, because we're not asking them to improve the course. Also coach fees, the
  internal positioning candour from `tma-product-facts.md`, and any coach names.
- **The source table is the sharpest thing in the pack.** Meta drives half the applications at 9%
  conversion while email converts at 44%. A candidate who spots that and says something about it is
  reading the data rather than decorating it.
- **The "one piece of actual work" deliverable** exists because the spec says this person markets it
  as well as builds it. It's the only part of the task that can't be reasoned about abstractly.
- **Rejected alternative:** a "how would you create the AI course" task. It's a blank page, so every
  submission would look plausible and none could be judged right or wrong, and it gives away
  strategy on a product that doesn't exist. Better as a live question at second stage, where you can
  push back in real time. Worth adding to the 2nd stage interview guide.
