# Blog / website examples (Up World "we" voice)

Pulled from the Up World blog (up-world.co/news-blog). These are the house format for talk and interview recaps: written in the collective "we" voice, bylined to the team (not Lottie's "I"). Use them as the model for blog posts and long-form web content.

## The house format (what every post does)

- A short, specific hook that names the guest and the single biggest lesson, no throat-clearing.
- Numbered insights, each with a sentence-case subhead, built around a real brand example and verbatim quotes from the guest.
- Concrete specifics and stats over adjectives (66% of the open market, Asda, £30k testing, one Instagram poll).
- A light sign-off pointing people to the full talk.
- Titles are specific and often a bit cheeky, occasional emoji: "How SURI outshone Oral-B on Trustpilot in just 2 years", "3 ways Grind turned coffee into culture".

## Full example: "What customer-obsessed brands actually do"

> Most marketers say they're consumer-centric. Far fewer operate as if a real human being is sitting in the meeting with them. This is the biggest lesson Debbie Epstein, Marketing Director at THIS!, hammered home during our UpTalk event.
>
> Below are the most powerful insights Debbie shared, with the real brand examples to show you how the brands Debbie has worked at (THIS!, Bounce, Ella's Kitchen) have put it all into practice.
>
> **1. You don't "already know your customer"**
> When Debbie joined Ella's Kitchen and asked who the core consumer was, the answer seemed confident: "A Surrey mum who has some disposable income." But the team couldn't explain why. So Debbie went into the sales and category data. "Asda was our fastest growing customer, and Surrey mums don't shop in Asda." That triggered a full segmentation project.
>
> **3. You don't need £30k demographic testing to be consumer-centric**
> At Bounce, Debbie's CEO wanted to sponsor football. Debbie felt the brand was more rugby-aligned. So she ran a simple Instagram pulse test: "Football or rugby?" Rugby won hands down. One poll settled what weeks of debate couldn't. Cheap insight can be transformational if you're brave enough to use it.
>
> [Close] Catch up on the full talk here.

What to notice: names the guest and the lesson up front, every point rides a real brand and a real quote, specific numbers throughout, ends by pointing to the talk rather than a hard CTA.

## Other real titles + hooks (the range)

- **How community marketing built this cult brand** (PureSport): "Most brands like the idea of building a brand community, but few know how to build one that feels real. The people are the community, not the brand. You just provide the place."
- **How SURI outshone Oral-B on Trustpilot in just 2 years**: "The dental aisle is dominated by giants like Oral-B and Philips. And yet SURI, founded in 2021, is now the highest-rated toothbrush brand on Trustpilot."
- **Comms advice to level-up your marketing, from Oddbox's CMO**: "If you've ever felt like your career zigzags more than it climbs, you're in good company."
- **How to get ahead of the social media algorithms for 2026** (ex-The North Face): "When a social strategist who's led content for The North Face and Starbucks says your playbook is outdated, it's well worth taking note."
