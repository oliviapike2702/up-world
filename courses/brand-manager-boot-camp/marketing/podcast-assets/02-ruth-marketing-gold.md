## Best quotes (cleaned of filler, verbatim meaning preserved)

- "Brand strategy is your who, what, how, and why." — Ruth's own plain definition: who you're
  talking to, what you're saying (positioning), why the company exists, then how you talk about it
  (messaging/marketing) — "you can't do the how until you've done the other pillars."
- "It should be very clear and very simple... a one sentence answer to all of those different
  elements." — Ruth, on what a good brand strategy actually looks like (not a deck, a page).
- "The best version of this is probably three bullet points that are incredibly precise in what you
  are and are not doing." — Lottie, on stripping brand strategy back to its essentials.
- "Commercial and strategic are the same thing... all of marketing is about driving sales in one way
  or another." — Ruth, quoting advice given to her that changed how she saw her own job.
- "Marketing needs to be seen as a growth engine, not a cost centre." — Ruth's central thesis on
  defending marketing's value internally.
- "You cannot over communicate those things... bring people from all over the business on the
  journey with you." — Ruth, on getting buy-in rather than expecting people to trust a "visionary"
  pitch on faith.
- The "old lady in the rocking chair" story — Ruth on becoming the sole brand guardian at Popchips
  after 8 years: "people would just be like, ask Ruth, ask Ruth." A vivid, self-deprecating image.
- "A huge shortcut to seniority is: how do I codify this, how do I teach it, so the culture knows
  what that looks like?" — Lottie, responding to the rocking-chair story — the risk of becoming a
  bottleneck "full-time approver" instead of building something that scales without you.

## Teaching takeaways (structured, each stands alone)

1. **Brand strategy = who, what, how, why — nothing more.** Who you're talking to, what you're
   saying (positioning), why the company exists, then how you talk about it. Skipping straight to
   "how" (the ads, the content) without the earlier pillars is why campaigns don't resonate.
2. **It's normal not to have a good brand strategy deck.** Ruth has worked on $100m+ brands with no
   real version of this document. The lesson isn't "wait for a perfect deck" — it's "write the one
   pager yourself."
3. **Real clarity beats a polished deck.** A stunning 20-slide deck that describes "everyone" as the
   target is worse than three precise bullet points about what you are and aren't doing.
4. **Keep it to 2–3 target audiences, max.** Use a "Venn diagram, aspirational centre of gravity"
   approach — find what your different segments have in common rather than writing a separate
   message for each one. Pen portraits (e.g. a named persona like "Sophie from Clapham") make this
   concrete for cross-functional teams, especially sales.
5. **Marketing has to actively defend its own value as a growth engine, not a cost centre.** This
   doesn't go away — even senior marketers have to keep proving the commercial link between clarity
   of messaging and conversion, buyer relationships, and sales.
6. **"Bring people on the journey"** means explaining the impact and the reasoning, not just
   announcing a decision — loop in sales especially, since they hold a different, valuable point of
   view on the target customer.
7. **A practical relationship-building habit**: Ruth had a standing monthly lunch with her sales
   counterpart at Popchips specifically to stay aligned on what marketing was prioritising and why.
8. **Watch out for becoming the sole "brand guardian."** Being the one person who instinctively knows
   what's on/off-brand feels valuable, but if it's not written down or taught, your own career growth
   gets capped — you become a full-time approver instead of someone who's built something that scales.
9. **Brand guidelines don't need a big design agency.** Ruth's Popchips example: simple "we are / we
   are not" tone-of-voice sliders with a line down the middle — cheap, clear, and effective.
