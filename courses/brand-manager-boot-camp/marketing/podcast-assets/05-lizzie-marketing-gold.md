# Podcast ep 1 — Lizzie Staiano on what being a brand manager really means — marketing gold

Source: `sources/transcripts/05-lizzie-raw.txt` (recorded [DATE PLACEHOLDER — confirm record date]).
Guest: Lizzie Staiano, Head of Marketing at TOTM (Time of the Month — organic period
care, no toxins or fragrances). Worked with Lottie for around 5 years at Brand Hackers before this
role, on distinct fractional projects. Been Head of Marketing at TOTM for about a year. Recorded for
the BMBC campaign.

Maps to **Module 1: What is a Brand** in the cohort calendar. Confirmed angle from Liv: what being a
brand manager really means, a day in the life, and how the role changes moving up from Brand Manager
through to Head of Marketing.

## The hook (usable as-is)
Lizzie is candid about something most people never admit: she felt "underwater" for the first three
months in her Head of Marketing role, despite years of experience. That honesty about the real,
unglamorous settling-in period is a strong, relatable hook, distinct from the other episodes.

## Best quotes (cleaned of filler, verbatim meaning preserved)

- "I just did not know about tampons before I started this job." — Lizzie, on doing a proper product
  induction despite years of marketing experience, a funny and disarming detail.
- "There is a moment at 90 days where you feel like you can breathe." — Lottie, naming the settling-in
  timeline explicitly.
- "How can we create the right world for our brand and how can we help the right people find it. That,
  for me, is something that doesn't really change across seniority." — Lizzie's own definition of
  what stays constant at every level of a marketing career.
- "Turn the feeling of crisis into a recommendation." — Lottie's sharp reframe of Lizzie's advice on
  handling chaos: don't just report a problem, bring the fix.
- "Will it make the boat go faster?" — Lizzie's simple decision-making test from a Brand Hackers
  leadership training, used to cut through chaos and prioritise.
- "If we're peaking professionally at 35... that is a long old decline." — Lottie, reframing career
  impatience: most people will work into their 80s, so peaking early isn't the win it looks like.

## Teaching takeaways (structured, each stands alone)

1. **The settling-in period is real and rarely admitted.** Lizzie describes feeling "underwater" for
   about three months in a new Head of role, even with years of experience and a strong track record.
   Lottie names two real checkpoints: 90 days (when you can finally breathe) and around a year (when
   things really click).
2. **How to actually get through it**: carve out real time to sit with the data and tools, read the
   strategy decks and tone of voice documents that came before you, do a proper product induction (no
   matter how experienced you are), sit with technical teams (she sat with in-house devs) and with
   customer-facing teams (she spent time with the customer care lead) to learn pain points directly,
   and give up the fear of asking "silly" questions.
3. **What actually changes with seniority, defined clearly:**
   - A Head of / Director sets the strategy: the 10,000-foot view, the KPIs, the shape of the whole
     year's plan. They report upward (justifying decisions to a CMO, CEO or board) and downward
     (translating the big strategy into what the team does day to day).
   - A Brand Manager works within that brief, delivering projects (copy, briefs, shoots) each quarter
     that ladder up to the bigger strategic goal.
4. **What stays exactly the same at every level**: everyone is a guardian of the brand. Protecting
   every touchpoint, the tone of voice, and keeping the customer in mind doesn't change whether you're
   a brand manager or a head of marketing, only the scope of what you're doing it across changes.
5. **The real fix for chaos and overwhelm**: don't just tell your manager you're struggling, bring a
   recommendation with it. "I haven't done this because I've got too much on, and my recommendation is
   we drop it because X matters more" is a genuinely usable script, and Lottie frames practising this,
   even imperfectly, as an early leadership skill.
6. **"Will it make the boat go faster?"** — a simple test for any decision made in the middle of
   chaos: does this specific piece of work actually move the business toward its goal, or is it just
   activity.
7. **Career patience, backed by a real analogy**: you can hold two truths at once. You don't need ten
   years of experience to get a shot at a role, but you also can't skip the actual reps needed to get
   good, the same way nobody expects a professional tennis player to skip the 10,000 hours. Around 80%
   of real learning happens on the job, not before it.
