## The campaign at a glance

Brand Manager Bootcamp is a 12-week programme for startup marketers stepping into or growing in a brand role. It ran twice a year for six years from 2019, paused, and is back rebuilt.

- **Runs:** 3 September to 18 November 2026 (12 weeks)
- **Format:** 8 live lectures, 4 live clinics with Brand Hackers, 8 podcast episodes, weekly worksheets, a Slack group, 3 months' free Up Club membership, a ticket to the Up Club Christmas Party
- **The clinics are technical deep dives, shaped by member demand:** CRM, paid ads, social, trade marketing
- **Price:** £899 early bird, then £999
- **Sign-up:** up-world.co/brand-manager-bootcamp
- **Delivered by:** Lottie, with Bee (Head of People) on Ways of Working and Your Career, and Brand Hackers experts on the clinics
- **Audience:** juniors (assistant brand managers, marketing execs, 0 to 5 years) and the heads of marketing and founders who send them

### Numbers, and how we talk about them

**Internal only:** 7 of 20 sold, 1 early-bird ticket left.

**In public:** half the cohort is gone (around 10 spots taken), and there's one early-bird place left before everyone pays £999. Do not use the real 7.

**Names we can drop as proof:** Gail's, Lee Stafford, Seep, Octopus Energy, Califia, Nonna Tonda are already in. Recognisable brands beat "20 marketers signed up" every time.

**Podcast guests we can name:** Lizzie Stainaio (Head of Marketing, Time of the Month) and Ruth Fittock. No one else is confirmed, so no other names in public yet.

### The story spine

Startups hand the broadest, hardest job in marketing to someone two years out of university, with none of the training P&G, Unilever and L'Oreal build for their grads. Those companies codified brand management decades ago. Most startups have one marketer, a Notion doc and whatever they can piece together from podcasts and LinkedIn. That gap is the whole reason BMBC exists. Every post points back to it from a different angle.

Lottie's "What Brand Management Actually Is" piece is the source text for the thesis. Lean on it.

## The posts

### Post 1 — Why it's back (build out loud)

**The story.** The honest account of why BMBC went away and why it's returned. It ran twice a year for six years and the results were real. Then the Up Club team shrank from five to two, and rather than run two courses at a 7 out of 10, Lottie put everything into the Marketing Accelerator (now on its seventh version). Two years of "when are you bringing it back" emails never stopped. Now the team's whole again, so it's back, rebuilt in-house, fully Lottie's, aiming for the 10 she always wanted.

**Why it works.** The "we paused it rather than run something mediocre" line is the kind of honesty Lottie's audience respects. A stronger flex than pretending it never left.

**Material we have.** All the above, true and cleared. Lottie's proposal comment is almost the copy already: "ran for years, results were incredible, paused to refocus, back by popular demand, Accelerator on v7, we know how to run courses that land."

**Capture / still needed.** The opener image: if Lottie has a real message saying something like "I think we need to do BMBC again", that screenshot is the post. Ask her for it.

**Leave out:** anything about the course previously being co-run with someone else. Keep it on Lottie and the team-resource reason.

### Post 2 — What it actually takes to be a great brand manager (build out loud)

**The story.** The thesis post. Most people think a brand manager "looks after the brand." The real job is holding the brand's vision in one hand and pulling every channel, campaign and piece of content towards it with the other, while the business moves at full speed underneath. It needs enough paid media to brief it, enough CRM to have a view on segmentation, enough creative judgment to push back on an agency, enough project management to keep six workstreams alive. And we routinely hand it to someone two years out of uni, with no one above them who was ever taught it either.

**Why it works.** It names the reader's real world, explains why the gap exists (big companies built training infrastructure, startups didn't), then lands on BMBC as the fix. It sells nothing directly and earns the right to sell later.

**Material we have.** Lottie's full "What Brand Management Actually Is" doc, in her voice. Pull straight from it.

**Capture / still needed.** Nothing. Ready to write.

Episodes still open (do not name people): a founder on what makes a great marketer, a marketer who navigated a big company as a junior, and the Your Career episode. ## Voice, quickly

You know Lottie's register better than this doc does, so just the guardrails: UK English, no em dashes, no emojis unless she uses one first, opinionated hook with a point of view rather than a warm-up line, short sentences, one story per post, the ask last and brief. Banned: delve, leverage, unlock, superpower, "it's worth noting", "here's the thing".
