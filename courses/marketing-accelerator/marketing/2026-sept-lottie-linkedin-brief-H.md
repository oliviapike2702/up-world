## What it's really for

The surface ask is 25 places. The real job is harder than that.

**25 is 19% above the best cohort ever.** TMA has landed 18, 19, 18, 20 and 21 across five cohorts.
Best-ever is 21. And this cohort has **one Lottie talk instead of two**, on time capacity, so the
conversion engine fires once.

the barrier isn't awareness, it's a marketer deciding to spend £1,490 of their own money.

someone paying it themselves. The story spine below does exactly that: it turns the price rise from a
thing to defend into the proof that the course gets rebuilt whenever someone says it isn't good enough.

## Story spine

**Leadership is never taught. So we teach it, and we keep rebuilding how we teach it every time
someone tells us it isn't good enough.**

Two halves, and every post points back to one of them.

**Half one, why the course exists.** Nobody teaches marketers to lead. Lottie ran into it herself at
24, running a team having had big business training they'd never been given. It's also the premise of
her talk. Posts 1 and 5.

**Half two, why it's worth £1,490.** Three receipts, all real:

1. **Attendance was dropping to ~60% by course end.** So they added the half-day IRL kick-off and put
   the price up 50%, from £990 to £1,490. Attendance has held above 90% ever since. Liv described it as
   a risk that became the best decision they made. (She first said 150%; confirmed as 50% on 30 Jul,
   which matches the £500 rise in `../admin/decisions.md`.)
2. **"The hour-long lectures were so jam-packed I didn't get to ask any questions."** So Lottie now
   does a 1-1 call with every person during the course.
3. **"I'm self-funding, I can't afford to pay in full."** So they built payment plans.

Posts 2, 3 and 4.

This is ownable, because no competitor can borrow it without the receipts. It's honest in the way the
briefing guide asks for, because each receipt starts with a complaint. And it's the only framing that
makes the price rise an argument for the course rather than an objection to it.

## Hand-off note

House rules for the posts themselves are in `../../../context/tov/linkedin-voice.md` and the
`lottie-linkedin` skill's voice guide. The ones most often broken:

- 1,250 to 3,000 characters. Under 1,250 reads as unfinished, not concise
- 14+ short paragraphs, most of them one line, each a real sentence
- Open on an opinionated hook or a confession. Never a rhetorical question, never a warm-up line
- **Close on a statement.** No closing question, no CTA line. Link in the body or first comment
- UK English, no em dashes, no hashtags, no sign-off. Emoji only where they do a job
- One genuinely sharp reframe per post. No defining by negation in any form
- **Lottie's copy isn't primarily about Lottie.** She's the frame, the reader is the subject

Never invent a fact about her. Her real career detail and anecdotes are in
