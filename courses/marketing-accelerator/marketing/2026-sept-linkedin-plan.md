## Why this plan exists

Four real TMA posts from the last cohort, reviewed by Liv on 30 Jul: *"they've all been really shit
in the past."* The engagement backs her up. **19,078 followers, and the posts got 2, 3 and 6
reactions.** That is 0.01 to 0.03%.

What was wrong, checked against `linkedin-voice.md`, which was written on 29 Jul 2026 and therefore
postdates every one of these posts:

| Problem | The rule it breaks |
|---|---|
| "How do we know we run a killer course?" / "What else do you need to hear? 😅" | §2, never open on a rhetorical question |
| Every post ends on a link | §6, close on a statement, no CTA |
| Post 1 opens on a testimonial before the reader cares | §5, earn the asset, land it at the end |
| "Because we ALL love a bulleted list", "In case you're new here and have NO clue" | §8, filler that apologises for taking up space |
| Post 1's CTA points at the new website, not the course | the link should serve the post's argument |
| "Early bird SOLD OUT" and "We're 70% off already" in consecutive lines | never publish a muddled offer |
| Posts run ~1,100 characters | §5, the 1,250 floor. Under it reads as a diary jotting |

**Two things did work and both get reused below:**
- The best-performing post (6 reactions, 5 comments) used **real screenshots of real messages with
  real people tagged.** Unfakeable proof beat every polished claim.
- The strongest argument in any of them was Dan Murray-Serter's ROI quote plus three commercial
  reasons aimed at the manager who sends someone. That is the Train your team case.

**The standing rule that shapes every post here:** the company page leads with something useful to the
reader, never with how good the course is. Lottie's own post that week opened *"Say 'I am a leader'
out loud. How does it feel?"* and made the reader do something. That is the bar.

## The mechanics, every post

From `linkedin-voice.md`. These are the ones the old posts got wrong:

- **1,250 to 3,000 characters.** Under 1,250 is the most common failure.
- **14+ short paragraphs**, most one line, generous white space.
- **Hook** surprises, names a problem they feel now, or promises a specific reward. Never a rhetorical
  question, never a warm-up line.
- **Close on a statement.** No closing question, no CTA line. The link goes in the body or the first
  comment, not as the finish.
- **No hashtags. No sign-off.** Emoji only as list markers, never decorative.
- **Teach something applicable within 24 hours**, grounded in a real named example.
- One deliberate reframe per post, as the turn. Never a negation pivot.
- **The post and that week's email need different entry points.** Same argument is fine, same opening
  is not.

## W2 · Tue 4 Aug · The training gap

> Most startups hire one marketer and give them none of the training a big business would.
>
> P&G, Unilever and L'Oreal spend years building brand managers. Frameworks, mentoring, a way of
> thinking that has been refined and handed down. Someone joins as a graduate and gets taught the craft
> by people whose job includes teaching it.
>
> A startup hires its first marketer, hands them the whole function, and hopes.
>
> That marketer is usually excellent. They are also making it up, because nobody built them a syllabus
> and there is no senior marketer above them to copy.
>
> We sent an email last week with the line "P&G trained their brand managers. Most startups just hired
> one." It got four times the clicks of anything else we sent that week, which tells you how many people
> recognised themselves in it.
>
> The gap shows up in specific places:
>
> 🔥 Making a call with half the data and having to defend it
> 🔥 Telling a founder no, with a reason that holds
> 🔥 Running someone more experienced than you
> 🔥 Turning "get better at leadership" into something you do on a Tuesday
>
> None of that is on a marketing course, because it is not marketing. It is the job you get handed the
> day you stop being the person doing the work.
>
> The Marketing Accelerator teaches both halves. 25 places, September to December.
>
> https://www.up-club.co/the-marketing-accelerator/
