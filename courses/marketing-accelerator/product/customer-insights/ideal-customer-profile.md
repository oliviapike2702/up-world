# Ideal Customer Profile

Built from the pattern analysis of all 14 screening calls. Companion to the Screening Call Insights tab: that one's for running a call, this one's for positioning and messaging.

## Section 1: Core applicant profile

| Attribute | Pattern |
| --- | --- |
| Company type | D2C/consumer brands dominate (food, drink, wine, hospitality). Steady B2B/tech minority. Occasional nonprofit, charity, or solo founder. |
| Team size | Solo marketer, or a team of one or two. Almost never inherits an existing marketing function, usually builds it. |
| Reporting line | A founder, MD, or CEO with no marketing background. This is the norm, not the exception. |
| Budget source | Company-funded when there's a supportive founder or CEO; self-funded or split with a payment plan when there isn't. Charity discount used where applicable. |
| Geography | UK-based, London-weighted for the in-person kickoff, but includes remote and international cohort members. |
| Company scale | Mostly startup/scale-up. One recurring exception: large, established businesses where the applicant is still operating in isolation despite the company's size. |

## Section 2: The decision-maker persona

Buyer and decision-maker are the same person here, sometimes with a manager's sign-off attached.

| Attribute | Pattern |
| --- | --- |
| Job titles | Marketing Manager, Brand Manager, Head of Marketing (acting or aspiring), Senior CRM Manager, Comms Lead |
| Judged on | Usually nothing concrete. No agreed KPIs or OKRs, output-based at best (an event delivered, a campaign run). The absence of a measurable framework is itself a pain point, not a footnote. |
| Reports to | A founder, MD, or CEO with no marketing background, on almost every call |
| Career stage | Doing marketing well, not yet seen as, or trained to be, a leader |

## Section 3: The situation and trigger

| Before state | What breaks |
| --- | --- |
| Doing marketing well, unsupported, no framework to measure against | The manager or mentor who trained them leaves, and the responsibility lands early |
| Competent but never formally trained to lead | They're stepped into a senior role ahead of schedule, no training attached |
| Employed, isolated, gap felt manageable | They leave, or have just left, full-time employment for freelance work, and the gap now feels urgent rather than optional |

## Section 4: Value mapping and proof points

| Pain point (their words) | How the Accelerator answers it | Proof point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "I know that I want leadership, but I don't think I'll be able to get it at this company." | A portable framework and a credential that travels with them, independent of one employer's internal politics | The course is application-only, built specifically for this stage, not tied to any single company's promotion path |
| "There might be gaps. Who knows?" | Group coaching sessions surface blind spots live, with a coach and peers, rather than leaving someone to find them alone | Consistently the single most-cited valuable part of the course, across nearly every past cohort |
| "I'm just kind of making it up." | The strategy modules give a repeatable framework (business case, brand strategy, growth and testing) to work from, instead of starting from zero each time | Structured around the exact gaps applicants describe unprompted: budgeting, business cases, brand strategy |
| "It's quite a lonely world." | A cohort of 20-25 people at the same career stage, plus the Up Club community that continues after the course ends | Past cohorts keep meeting up long after finishing, unprompted |
| "My skill set is still amazingly there. But my leadership confidence is not anymore." | The course is explicit that this isn't a skills gap. It's built to rebuild confidence and backing yourself, not to re-teach marketing | Repeated almost verbatim by the team on every screening call: "they know their shit... the problem is they can't back themselves" |

## Section 6: The anti-ICP

Looks like a fit on paper, doesn't land in the cohort. Declining rarely means a hard no here, usually a redirect to Brand Manager Bootcamp or a deferral to the next cohort. But knowing the difference matters: one mismatched person changes the dynamic for the other 20 to 24 people who paid for a room full of peers at the same stage.

**Characteristics**

| Type | Why it doesn't work |
| --- | --- |
| Wants tactics, not leadership | Asking for growth hacks, paid ads playbooks, follower counts. This course teaches strategic thinking, not channel execution. |
| Still learning the fundamentals | Hasn't yet reached the "doing marketing well" stage the course assumes. Needs Brand Manager Bootcamp first. |
| Corporate marketer, big team, real budget | No scrappy, resource-constrained problem to solve. The shoestring-budget content won't land, and they won't relate to the room. |
| Applying for the credential, not the gap | No real felt tension in their current role, no story of being overlooked or thrust in. Wants the badge, not the change. |
| Timing wrong more than fit wrong | Mid-job-search with no clear direction, about to change roles entirely, or too much personal upheaval to commit to weekly sessions. |

**Red flags to listen for on the call**

- Asks how to get X% growth or run better ads, rather than how to lead or be taken seriously
- Describes their own gap only in fundamentals language ("I want to learn what a marketing strategy is")
- Can't name a specific tension in their current role, everything sounds smooth, no friction, no story
- Already one of several marketers in an established, well-resourced team, not the most senior or the only voice
- Vague or non-committal about whether they'll actually show up, given everything else going on

**Why it's worth declining or deferring**

- Group coaching depends on everyone being at the same career crossroads. One mismatch shifts the dynamic for the whole group.
- Someone who isn't genuinely at this stage is also the most likely to disengage once the novelty wears off.
- Word of mouth is the engine here. A person who didn't get value is the story they'll tell afterward.
