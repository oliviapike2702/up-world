# The Marketing Accelerator — curriculum

Source: "The Marketing Accelerator Workbook" (crib notes), March 2026 module folders (slide decks
where extractable, podcast transcripts), Handover Jan 2026 doc. Module 3 (Efficient Spend) slide
decks were binary/too large to extract — that module's detail below comes from crib notes and
podcast only, thinner than the others.

## Module 1 — Marketing Strategy
Hosted by Lottie. Podcast: Sam, Founder of Pot Gang.
- Understanding commercial context: questions to ask CEO/finance/board about funding and ambition
- Cheat sheet: what the business needs from marketing vs. how marketing is delivering (score /10) vs. what to change
- The 5Ps self-scoring exercise (Product, Price, Promotion, People, Place)
- STOP / START / CONTINUE exercise
- "WHO INSPIRED BUSINESS CHALLENGE" (WIBC) framework — `GET XXX, WHO USED TO THINK/DO/FEEL YYY, TO DO ZZZ`.
  This recurs as the backbone framework across nearly every later module.
- Marketing-focused SWOT: risks/opportunities to the business and who controls them; stakeholder mapping
- Goal-setting table: Goal → what it unlocks → what that unlocks
- Turning WIBCs into Strategy + Tactics

## Module 2 — Building a Brand
Hosted by Lottie. Podcast/lecture guest: Charlotte Langley, VP Brand & Comms, Bloom & Wild.
- Definition: "Your brand (IDENTITY) is what other people say about you when you're not in the room."
- "Brand building" is a lazy phrase — 5 distinct objectives, must choose between them: build
  awareness / engagement / advocacy / perception / CHANGE perception (each has a distinct mechanic)
- Brand health funnel: Awareness → Interest → Desire → Action → Loyalty (scored /10)
- Brand identity exercise: "We exist to… / We sell… / Our personality is… / We stand against…"
- Worked case example: Sula wine (Indian market) — two divergent brand-growth challenges resolved
  via a single brand territory ("Freedom to explore")
- Budget-forced-choice exercise: 3x ✅ and 5x ❌ distributed across the 5 objectives, no ties allowed
- Brand building on a budget: mapped against every customer touchpoint
- Measuring brand success: get clear on the "story" (what a customer will say/do) before reaching for numbers
- Podcast headline (Charlotte Langley): risk isn't spending too little on brand, it's "spreading your budget too thin"

## Module 3 — Efficient Spend / "Big Reach, Tiny Budgets"
Hosted by Andy Shovel, Founder of THIS.
- Framing: WIBC → pick a campaign objective from SELL / AWARENESS / NEW CUSTOMERS / ALWAYS
  ON-AUTHORITY / DATA COLLECTION / FEEDBACK / PRODUCT / F**K UP
- 12-point customer-knowledge checklist (age, salary, living status, work status, kids, hobbies,
  media consumption, weekend behaviour, where to find them, lifestyle/travel/diet) + self-score 1–10
- Measuring success menu: emails collected, awareness lift, purchase intent, social engagement,
  NPS/Trustpilot, coverage, website traffic, conversions
- "Finding your brand's mojo": audit channels/messaging/imagery/formats that already resonate
  (worked example: Strava/TikTok, founder-led comms with emojis, hands-in-lifestyle imagery, listicles)
- Negotiating on a startup budget: non-financial leverage — free stock, audience exposure,
  LinkedIn posts, team time, friends & family networks
- Podcast headline (Andy Shovel): "good marketing makes someone feel something, mediocre marketing
  does not"; his "acid test" for judging an idea; £6-7k video hitting 500k views as a real example

## Module 4 — Growth & Testing
Hosted by James, Head of Brand Hackers (ex-innocent, ex-BrewDog). Podcast: Aiste Juknaite, ex-Director of Growth, Gorillas.
- Reframes growth hacking away from "hack"/"sketchy trick" — lumberjack metaphor: clear goal,
  high-impact strikes, iterate technique
- Core loop: draw hypotheses → test variables → analyse results → double down on winners → repeat
- Everyone is a growth marketer, not just those with "growth" in the title
- Sean Ellis scale exercise: self-score from "what's a test?" (1) to full growth mindset (5)
- Hypothesis format: "Given [our current data], we believe taking [this action] will impact
  [these results] by [this much]"
- 5-step experiment process: choose area → hypothesis → design experiment → run to significance → analyse
- ICE framework for prioritising experiment ideas
- AARRR framework (Dave McClure/500 Startups): Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue
- Podcast headline (Aiste Juknaite): growth thinking is the antithesis of the "creative/chaotic
  marketer" stereotype — it's structured and scales

## Module 5 — Operating in a Business
Hosted by Emma Roberts, Senior Director at Lean In.
- Framing: "The more senior you get, the less marketing you do" — the shift from doing marketing to leading it
- Zero-based budgeting, 4 steps: (1) list every goal with deadline + quantifiable measure,
  (2) shared spreadsheet with budget/resource/rationale columns, (3) reorder by true priority
  ("if I could only do ONE thing"), (4) present via the SUMMARY tab, thinking about what the
  audience (e.g. finance) actually worries about
- AARRR reappears mapped to WIBC/brand-identity/growth-experiment tools — course deliberately
  re-threads earlier frameworks
- "Get to know your finance decision-maker" exercise (reporting schedule, motivations, time spent
  with them) — repeated for "who is your boss really"
- HBR's four workplace drives referenced: acquire, bond, comprehend, defend
- Influencing framework: reuse WIBC + Emma Roberts' business-case-building method
- Podcast headline (Emma Roberts): "you need to learn to RELISH difficult questions"

## Module 6 — Leadership & Working with Founders
Podcast guests: Luke Boase, Founder Lucky Saint; Rachel Carrell, Founder Koru Kids.
- Opens with self-scored definition-of-success clarity (1–10, "never thought about it" →
  "written down and reviewed quarterly")
- 15-item "success is…" prompt menu (wealth/power, wisdom, mindfulness, self-confidence, growth,
  giving back, balance, values, community, pride, boundaries, lifelong learning, resilience,
  interests, feeling good about choices)
- Leadership role-model exercise: two worked examples in the deck itself — "my first ever boss"
  and "my mum" — scored on what to emulate vs. avoid
- Distil into 3 words for "the leader you want to be" — pushed to be non-generic
- Leadership theory fragment: "a leader is not a manager / doesn't have to be in charge / is a
  grown-up / has an agenda to lead"
- "Manifesto" exercise: 3-month personal (not strategic) goals via DONE THE WORK / VISION / COMMUNICATED
- Podcast headline (Luke Boase): founders want a recommendation and "5 simple bullet points" from their marketing team
- Podcast headline (Rachel Carrell): leadership as fundamentally about transparency

## Bonus Lecture — Getting to Know Your Customer
- Reiterates WIBC framework
- Self-score 1 ("honestly making it up") to 5 ("I chat to them weekly") on customer knowledge
- Real-world inspiration examples: HelloFresh's quarterly "segment safaris," M&S head office doing
  a full day in-store yearly, FloodFlash joining sales calls regularly
- "Insight" defined as: "I want to think/do/feel XXX BUT YYY" — "we're dealing in emotions not
  facts"; warns "my target audience is everyone" is where you'll go wrong
- Total Addressable Market vs. Target Customer vs. Prime Prospect (an individual who directs all comms)
- Two research approaches: deep-dive projects vs. integrated into ways of working
- Tech stack recommended verbatim: Typeform (own customers) + Claude/ChatGPT for analysis;
  Pollfish or friends & family for other audiences; Calendly/Zoom/Meets + Descript AI editing for
  interviews; userinterviews.com for recruiting
- Survey-writing rules: define objectives first, don't stuff the survey, never ask leading
  questions, cut anything that won't move the needle
- Full customer journey mapped to research method at each stage (automated DMs, Hotjar exit
  surveys, welcome-flow surveys, KnoCommerce post-purchase survey, onboarding interviews, product
  feedback flows)
- Other listening channels: sales teams, customer service, social listening, standing in store,
  trade shows, mystery-shopping competitors
