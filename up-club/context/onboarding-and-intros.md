# Onboarding calls & new member intros

Harvested 8 July 2026 from the Liv Handover doc (Jan 2026).

## Why onboarding calls exist

- Members feel overwhelmed when they join; the calls show them around.
- Members want to connect with other members immediately, so calls are split **freelancer vs in-house** so peers meet peers from day one.

After the call a member should feel: excited to get stuck into the resources, inspired by members they've already met, and like they're in a safe space. Concretely they should leave knowing how to sign up to events, use the members area and Slack, and holding **3 actions** to get stuck in.

## The 30-minute call structure

1. **First 5 mins** — warm welcome, everyone intros themselves and why they joined (helps tailor the rest).
2. **10 mins — key features**, screen-sharing: the members area, then Slack (highlight a couple of channels, always #askthecommunity).
3. **10 mins — their questions**, steering toward: get a mentor, join or host a Huddle Up, list in the Black Book, update your profile.
4. Also gather research: what events do you want in 2026? why did you join? what does your dream Up Club experience look like? Write insights up afterwards (they feed an insights doc).

### Tailoring by member type

- **Freelancers/agencies** want to know how to get in front of clients. The line that works: "keep yourself top of mind for brand-side members by being open and generous with advice, no strings attached". How: host a Huddle Up, get listed in the Black Book, be active in #askthecommunity, offer no-strings coffee chats.
- **Brand side** want to hear from people killing it, get mentors, meet peers in their industry. Show the Huddle Up line-up and how to sign up, and the mentoring page.

## New member intros (matching)

Every new member gets an intro to a like-minded member within their first month — it makes the community immediately valuable and sticky.

How matching works (Google Sheets, one for in-house and one for freelancers):

- **Same seniority**: execs with execs, managers with managers, heads-of with heads-of. Mixing levels turns it into mentoring, which isn't the point here.
- **Same industry and role**: don't match B2B tech with FMCG/D2C; match social media managers with social media managers, growth with growth.
- **Their stated preference wins**: the "who do you want to be matched with" column is the primary guide.
- Work the easy matches first, then the niche requests (search the skills column for things like "international market expansion").

Intro copy pattern (Slack, tag both + their LinkedIns): warm two-line intro of each person with role and company, one line on why they're a great match, sign off light. AI could do a decent first pass at matching but hasn't yet been tuned well enough (as of Jan 2026).
