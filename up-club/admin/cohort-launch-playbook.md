# Up Club — cohort launch playbook

Source: Google Drive cohort folders (Spring/March 2025, Beta/June-July 2025, September 2025, May
2026) + review decks, pulled 2026-07-22. This is a narrative history and set of learnings, not
yet a clean step-by-step checklist — see "Not yet pulled in" below.

## Why cohorts at all
Up Club struggled with acquisition for ~2 years before 2024 — people knew about it but had no
urgency to join. Switched to a cohort ("doors open/close") structure in Jan 2024 to create
scarcity. Runs 3x/year: January, May, September. Doors typically open for about a week; a longer
marketing run-up of 4-6 weeks precedes it.

## Cohort-by-cohort history

### March/Spring 2025 — first cohort under the new structure
New landing pages ("For Marketers"/"For Teams"), new members area, full website redesign, Meta
Ads (11 Mar-~8 Apr), doors open 14-22 April.

**Result: disastrous.** £4,034 ad spend, 30 waitlisters total, only 1 paid customer (3.3%
waitlist→paid conversion), ROAS 0.1. Missed target by 30K.

**Diagnosed causes:** offer wasn't tight/compelling enough for brand marketers (vs. freelancers,
where ROI is obvious); nurture flow too salesy and too long; ads pulled 6 days early; not enough ad
creative testing; too reliant on 1-2 channels.

### June/July 2025 "Beta Launch" — secret, small-scale relaunch to test fixes before September
Explicit decision not to wait for a full Sept launch — ran a "secret" 50-Marketing-Manager beta,
23-27 June sign-up window, membership kicked off 30 June. Leaned into WhatsApp/LinkedIn DMs over
email; introduced a ~£30 deposit function; gift-sending (Radical Candor book or Cardly postcard,
50:50 split test); manual outreach via a shared tracker sheet with same-day contact SLA. New
100-day/personal-development proposition tested on the landing page.

**Result: quality over quantity, real signal found.** Waitlist→paid conversion jumped from 12%
(April) to 53-56% (June) — the deposit function specifically drove ~90% conversion among those who
paid it. Meta ads nearly profitable (ROAS 0.2 → 1.13; CAC £2,019 → £354). Still only 39
waitlisters total (much smaller volume than April's 360).

**Learnings carried forward:** the deposit "bite" works; top-of-funnel is still the core unsolved
problem; Lottie's personal LinkedIn drives ~50% of new members and deserves dedicated investment;
the 10K-subscriber email database is underused; email copy briefs to the copywriter were poor
quality (Liv "isn't an email marketer and doesn't enjoy doing it," emails landing "5/10,"
copywriter over-relying on ChatGPT). Also floated here: the Personal Development workshop (see
`../events/irl/pd-workshop.md`) and a possible new "Agency partner" tier (£3k/yr, target £12K
revenue in 2025).

### September 2025 — target hit
Target lowered from April's unrealistic 190 new members to 100 new members / £13K — **hit this
time** (100 new members, £13K). Still missing an agency-revenue target of £7.5K for the year;
several promising agency leads went cold.

New consolidated landing page + thank-you page raised waitlist conversion 4.88%→5.74% (+17.6%).

**Meta ads run one more time as "one last attempt":** £323 spend, 3 waitlisters, 1 paid customer —
roughly breakeven, pulled for cashflow reasons. Effectively a failed channel three cohorts running
(spring/beta/Sept all ~£2-4K spend, near-zero ROAS on straight Meta ads without a very tight offer).

**LinkedIn boosted posts were the standout:** £1,123 spend, 640 landing views, 16-26 paid customers
(attribution between organic/paid isn't fully clean, but implied ROAS is very high).

**Expandi (LinkedIn DM outreach) replaced PhantomBuster** for proper analytics: £74 spend, 2 paid
customers; connection rate good (27%) but conversation rate poor (9.5%) — opening message was too
hard-pitch.

**Company-wide context:** member count has been stuck around 1,000 for ~2 years (structural
ceiling), but revenue is rising because retention/LTV is improving — churn ~5.5% (2025) vs 6.5%
(2024). One exception: July cohort's M3 retention dropped to 78% (vs typical 90%+ target),
attributed largely to freelancer churn (cost-sensitivity, low usage, "thought there'd be more
freelance opportunities than there was").

**Channel-mix conclusion (reasserted here and elsewhere):** referrals + Lottie's LinkedIn together
drive the majority of new members — one deck breaks this as 50% from Lottie's LinkedIn, 20%
referred by non-members; ~60-70% of new members were never on the waitlist at all, underscoring
"on-the-week" hustle over the pre-built list.

**Actions logged for next round:** always-on cold email campaign, shift mindset from "marketing Up
Club" to "selling Up Club" (sales calls, fast lead response), tighten the freelancer proposition
(too many low-value freelancers churning), possible move to limited quarterly-vs-annual membership
availability.

### May 2026 — a clear miss, root-caused to top-of-funnel
Planning now formalized into phases: Planning (23 Mar-10 Apr) → Marketing (13 Apr-22 May) → Doors
open (25-29 May). Target: 120 new members, deliberately no "big marketing stunt" — just refine
existing channels + tighten proposition.

**Result: 60 new members, 51 waitlisters — missed.** CVR fell to 3% vs ~7% in Sept 2025/Jan 2026.

**Root cause identified:** website traffic during the open week was 50% lower than prior cohorts
(301 views vs 618-697) — a genuine top-of-funnel/awareness shortfall, not a conversion problem
(on-site conversion was actually fine, 9% for non-waitlist members).

Waitlist conversion also slipped: 75% actual vs 90% goal; 10 people never responded on any
channel; spam-folder issues surfaced ("a lot of people said we were falling into spam, had to rely
heavy on WhatsApp") — ties to the known up-world.co domain deliverability issue (suppressing open
rates workspace-wide since early 2026; fix expected ~late July 2026).

**Meta ads, tighter "Up Fest" cross-sell offer:** best CVR yet (0.2%, "4x better than Sept"), but
heavy drop-off at the Typeform stage — conclusion is still "need better data capture/nurture for
cold warm leads," not "ads finally work." **`meta-ads-for-up-club-march-2026`** (a Drive doc, not
yet pulled into this workspace) tracks this channel test-by-test from March 2025 through May 2026
and states the running verdict plainly: **three consecutive cohorts of Meta ads have failed**, and
the working theory each time is offer/proposition tightness, not creative or targeting.

**Talks/IRL as acquisition, tested this round:** "James' AI talk" (54 signups, 25 attended, 3
became members, 12%) and a second talk (38 signups, 22 attended, 4 became members, 14%) — both
roughly half the signups of Lottie's previous best-performing talk, but conversion held up.

**What worked:** when Lottie's own LinkedIn DMing hit friction, switching outreach to come from Liv
instead (for warm leads/prospective calls) converted 5/5 calls.

**Forward actions logged:** fix domain/deliverability before September; set up WhatsApp Business
for warm-lead nurture and onboarding; keep the £30 deposit mechanic; invite waitlisters to all
public Up Talks pre-doors-open to keep them warm; consider bringing in a B2B tracking expert to
properly validate ad attribution.

## Reusable nurture email skeleton
Found in "Email Marketing Flow May 2026": a 6-week nurture sequence — Pattern Interrupt → Reframe
→ Reality Check → Proof → Commitment/Deposit-explainer → Urgency/scarcity week. This is the
closest thing to a reusable nurture template in the whole corpus — worth lifting wholesale into
future launches rather than rebuilding each time.
