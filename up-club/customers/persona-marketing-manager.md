# Up Club member persona — Marketing Manager

`[SOLID]` — sourced from `../../context/audience/Up Club pain points - marketing managers.md`
and `../../context/audience/Up Club positioning research - leaders and managers.md`.

## Snapshot
- Marketing Manager at a D2C startup, aged 25–35
- Solo marketer, or part of a team of 2–3
- Reports directly to Founder, MD, or Head of Marketing
- Manages junior team members and cares about their development
- Limited budget and resources; ambitious, proactive, loves self-development
- Unlikely to pay for the membership themselves — needs to make the case to their manager

## Jobs to be done — why they join
- Accelerate their career, become more strategic, build leadership skills
- Learn from top industry leaders, get reliable advice fast
- Demonstrate ambition to their manager
- Build a powerful professional network, connect with a mentor
- Feel less alone

## Top pain points (verbatim quotes)
- "I'm being asked to deliver results like a CMO, but I have no one to bounce ideas off or validate my decisions."
- "There's a new platform or trend every week. If I fall behind once, I might never catch up."
- "I've never been properly trained. I'm making it up as I go and praying it works."
- "I'm always firefighting — there's no time or space to actually learn and grow."
- "It feels like everyone else has mentors and connections. Why don't I?"

## Core emotional insight
The deepest pain isn't a knowledge gap — it's **professional loneliness**. They work alone,
wear too many hats, have no one to sanity-check ideas with, and quietly Google everything while
pretending they've got it figured out. They're not buying education; they're buying confidence,
belonging, validation, and a safety net.

## Objections that keep them out
- Uncertainty over what Up Club actually delivers ("vibey network" perception)
- Tight/no personal budget — needs a case for their manager
- Apathy without a trigger (FOMO/scarcity helps)

## Messaging angles that land
- Career acceleration — "become the marketer everyone wants to hire"
- Community — "stop doing marketing alone"
- Confidence — "replace guesswork with guidance"
- Belonging — "finally find people who understand exactly what your job feels like"
