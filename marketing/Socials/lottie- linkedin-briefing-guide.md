# Brand Hackers — LinkedIn Briefing Guide

## Stop briefing posts. Start briefing campaigns.

A post asking people to buy a ticket is an advert. A campaign that takes them behind the scenes, into the drama, and on the journey — that's storytelling. Here's how we need to shift.

| How it's been briefed | How it needs to work |
|---|---|
| ✕ "Can you post about our event next week?" | → A series of posts that build anticipation and trust |
| ✕ One post. One ask. Buy the ticket. | → BTS, decision-making, drama, launch, reflection |
| ✕ Briefed days before the deadline | → Briefed weeks — ideally months — in advance |
| ✕ Idea comes from me, not from you | → Ideas come from you, shaped with me in a call |
| ✕ Content is transactional: here's the link | → Content earns attention before it asks for anything |
| ✕ Nothing before, nothing after | → The campaign tells the whole story |

---

## The Campaign Arc — every launch needs this

**01. Build out loud**
BTS photos, early decisions, the messy middle. Show the work before it's finished.

**02. Make them decide**
Ask the audience. "Help me choose the menu." "Which speaker should we invite?" Pull them in.

**03. The launch moment**
Now — and only now — ask. The ticket, the sign-up, the link. They've earned it. So have you.

**04. Be honest after**
Reflect. What sold out? What didn't? What went wrong? Honesty builds trust for next time.

## Worked Example — Up Club Cohort Launch

What this actually looks like in practice:

1. BTS screenshots from the Slack channel as the team builds the cohort — the chaos, the excitement, the thread that goes nowhere and then suddenly clicks.
2. A photo of the team sat around a laptop at the launch moment — "We're going live in 10 minutes and someone's wifi just dropped."
3. Lottie asking the audience to vote between two interview formats for the Big Up Club sessions. Let them co-create it.
4. Screengrab of early interview prep notes or a voice note transcription — the unglamorous bit that makes the glamorous bit possible.
5. Launch post — cohort is open, here's the link, here's what's in it.
6. Honest post-launch: "We had X spots. Here's what surprised us." Or: "It didn't sell the way we hoped. Here's what we learned."

## The Non-Negotiables

**Brief me early — not the week before**
A campaign needs time to breathe. BTS content can only exist if someone's capturing it. That planning needs to start at the start, not at the deadline.

**The ideas need to come from you**
You know your project. What's the drama? What decision did you agonise over? What moment would surprise people? Bring those ideas to our call.

**Capture as you go**
Screenshots of the Slack. A photo of the planning meeting. A voice note about a decision. You don't need a photographer — you need the habit of saving the moment.

**Earn the ask before you make it**
Nobody buys a ticket from a stranger. The campaign builds familiarity, trust, and desire — so that when you finally say "sign up", they already want to.
